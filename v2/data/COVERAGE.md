## Nationwide re-tabulation (builds 202610141482 to 202610141490)

**Boundaries.** All years use the current (2024) district boundaries. Results are re-tabulated to the current districts:
- **2020:** VEST 2020 precincts, area-weighted into the current districts.
- **2024:** official county totals for districts made of whole counties. Elsewhere, precincts are area-weighted into the districts.

**2024 precincts.** These now come from The New York Times 2024 presidential precinct results and boundaries (github.com/nytimes/presidential-precinct-map-2024, NYT C-UDA non-commercial license, with credit). The NYT data is merged per county with the earlier Census VTD + MEDSL data, keeping whichever matches the official county total better. Alaska keeps the official Division of Elections file. Examples:
- Florida: 61 of 67 counties are now within 2% of the official totals (28 before). Manatee now has 71 precincts at 99.6% of the official total (61 precincts at 85% before).
- Alabama: 49 of 67 counties are now within 2% (no file before).
- Statewide files were added for CT, DE, HI, MA, MO, NJ, ND, RI and WI. CO, WY and NE were completed.

**How the districts are filled:**
- Whole counties use the official county totals.
- Split counties use precincts, area-weighted. Before that, each county's precincts must sum to 70–100% of the official total and cover at least 70% of the county's area. Precincts are scaled per party to the official county totals.
- Rows filled this way carry the flag `est` (≈ estimate).
- **Validation:** I checked against districts with known results, using the final thresholds, on 18,827 districts. Median total error is 0.71%, median Republican-share error is 0.06 points, and the 90th-percentile Republican-share error is 1.02 points.

## Town rows in list-all drill-down: 'no data' audit (build 1527)

**Root cause (Idaho and most states).** The town polygons exist for every listed town, and 2024 precinct data exists in every Idaho county. The town value was computed by summing the precincts whose *centroid* lies inside the town polygon. A town that is smaller than the precinct(s) it sits in (typical for rural Idaho, e.g. Council and New Meadows are inside 5 county-wide precincts) has no precinct centroid inside it, so it showed 'no data'. Kamiah only worked because a precinct centroid happened to fall inside the city.

**Fix.** When no precinct centroid is inside the town, the town's result is the real results of the precinct(s) that overlap it, weighted by the share of the *town's* area lying in each precinct (28x28 sample grid, at least half of the town's area must be covered by precinct polygons; otherwise it stays 'no data'). Shares are the area-weighted precinct shares. Counts are the overlapping precincts' totals pro-rated the same way, so they are an upper bound for the town (a precinct also holds voters outside the town). These rows carry the approximate mark (≈) in lists. Towns that contain at least one precinct centroid are unchanged (exact, no mark). No precinct or number is invented: no overlapping precinct means no data.

2020 is a separate gap: no 2020 precinct file exists for most Idaho counties (only Ada, Blaine, Bonner, Butte, Caribou, Kootenai have one), so 2020 and delta rows for towns elsewhere stay 'no data' until 2020 precinct data is added (same for the states below).

## Iowa townships added to the town layer (Oct 18, 2026, branch `data/geo`, no app/BUILD change)

Iowa's town layer had 60 city rows (v1 `data/Iowa.js`) plus 1,011 city-part rows (`v2/data/parts/Iowa.js`) but **none of its 1,598 townships**. TIGER 2024 county subdivisions for Iowa (`tl_2024_19_cousub.zip`, Census, public domain; FUNCSTAT G = township) were appended to `v2/data/parts/Iowa.js` with `tools/add_tiger_towns.py` as `[county, NAMELSAD, polygons]` rows (county names from the Census 2024 Gazetteer).

- **Geometry:** the v1 town-row convention (buffer(0), simplify 0.0002°, ×10000 deltas; at this tolerance the 60 Iowa city rows rebuilt from TIGER match the v1 rows' vertex counts, median ratio 1.00). 1,598 rows, 30,396 vertices in total (19 per row).
- **Results:** none stored; as for every town row, the app computes them at runtime from `data/precincts20XX/Iowa.js` (sum of precincts whose centroid lies inside, else its area-weighted ≈ estimate). Nothing invented.
- **Size: `v2/data/parts/Iowa.js` 59,591 → 339,540 bytes (+279,949, 5.7×).** About 217 KB of that is geometry. The file is lazy-loaded when Iowa towns are opened. At 0.0005° the geometry would be about 155 KB and at 0.001° about 133 KB (townships are mostly 6×6-mile squares); the existing convention was kept, so a coarser tolerance needs the orchestrator to decide.
- **173 townships are hidden by the app**: `townsIn()` in `v2/index.html` drops a parts row when `normT()` (name with the city/town/township/village… suffix removed) equals a town already listed in that county, e.g. Adair County "Greenfield township" behind "Greenfield city". So 1,425 townships show now and 173 will show once the app's de-duplication also compares the suffix. The same rule already hides other rows in other states (counts from `tools/coverage_audit.py`): IL 473, NY 197, KS 154, MI 100, OH 83, IN 63, NE 54, MO 33, CT 23, VT 15, MN 1 (mostly villages and cities behind same-named townships).

## Iowa townships re-simplified at 0.0005° with shared edges (Oct 18, 2026, branch `data/geo`)

The 1,598 Iowa township rows in `v2/data/parts/Iowa.js` were re-simplified at 0.0005° (orchestrator decision) with `tools/resimplify_towns.py`. Simplifying each polygon on its own leaves neighbours with slightly different copies of the same edge (gaps and overlaps). Instead, the whole TIGER 2024 Iowa county-subdivision coverage (townships + city MCDs) was split into shared arcs, each arc was Douglas-Peucker simplified once at 0.0005° (end nodes fixed), and the faces were rebuilt and given back to their township. ## Points (Oct 18, 2026, branch `data/geo`): star location for the state icon beside every geo-list row
Files `v2/data/points/<State>.js` (STATE_FILES names; DC is `District-of-Columbia.js`), built by `tools/build_points.py`, checked by `tools/check_points.py`.
Format: `__points("State",{county:{key:[lon,lat]},town:{...},place:{...},sch:{...},cd:{...},sldu:{...},sldl:{...}});` in WGS84 degrees, the same space the icon code's star already takes (`window.__stSil(st,[lon,lat])` projects it with the silhouette's own projection).
Keys are the row keys without the leading `State|`: county = NAT row name, town = `County|Name` (all parts rows + v1 town rows), place = places row name, sch = school row name, cd/sldu/sldl = row code. For the 86 v1 rows (`states/<State>.js` / root data) whose key repeats in one state (74 school, 11 place, 1 town), the plain key goes to the row that matches the newer unique-keyed row, and each other row also gets an entry keyed by its TIGER 2024 GEOID (e.g. Ohio town `3915981242`, second Washington township of Union County; the parts file already names that row `Washington township (3915981242)`, which has its own entry).
Rule: TIGER 2024 Gazetteer INTPT when it falls inside every shape the app holds for the row (state file and national file), else polylabel of the shapes' intersection. Inside means shapely contains, more than 1e-7 deg off the edge, and the app's even-odd ray test. 3 decimals; 2 decimals for Illinois where still inside, to keep it under 60 KB gzip; 4 to 5 decimals for 65 tiny town rows.

## ZIP cross-reference (Oct 18, 2026, branch `data/geo`): ZCTA → place / town / county rows
Files: `v2/data/zcta_xref/<first 3 ZIP digits>.js`, 891 files. Built by `tools/build_zcta_xref.py` from the Census 2020 ZCTA relationship files (`tab20_zcta520_{place20,cousub20,county20}_natl.txt`).
Format: `__zxref("194",{"19405":{st:"PA",place:[[key,share]],town:[[key,share]],county:[[key,share]]},...});`
- Keys are the geo-list keys within state `st`: the place row name, the town key `County|Name`, and the NAT county name.
- An entry in a different state (137 ZCTAs cross a state line) carries a third element with that state, e.g. `[key,share,"NJ"]`.
- The share is the part of the ZCTA's land area, to 2 decimals, sorted largest first. Parts under 1% are dropped unless nothing else is left.
- Coverage: 33,642 ZCTAs (all 50 states + DC; Puerto Rico is not in v2). 33,642 have counties, 22,957 have places and 15,463 have towns. Towns only exist where v2 has town rows (MCD states).
- Connecticut counties are summed from the town parts by 2024 planning region, because the 2020 county file still uses the old counties.

Not listed, with reasons:
- CDPs: not v2 place rows.
- Statistical CCDs and unorganized territories: not v2 town rows.
- 93 incorporated 2020 places and MCDs that no longer exist in 2024, so they have no v2 row. Examples: Bethel Heights AR (merged into Springdale), Cahokia, Alorton and Centreville IL (merged into Cahokia Heights), the dissolved Nebraska townships, WI towns Greenville, Rib Mountain and Madison (incorporated or dissolved), and DC "Washington city" (DC has no place rows). The full list is in `../geo-data-raw/zcta_xref_report.json` (`unmapped_incorporated`).
- Renames between 2020 and 2024 are mapped: MA "X Town city" → "X city", TX "Pecos city" → "Town of Pecos city".

Example: 19405 = place Bridgeport borough 0.56; towns Montgomery County|Bridgeport borough 0.56 and Montgomery County|Upper Merion township 0.44; county Montgomery County 1.0.
Sizes: total 5.09 MB raw / 901 KB gzip. Per file: min 88 B / 93 B, median 3.7 KB / 691 B, max 29.1 KB / 4.8 KB (prefix 562).

## Geo search index (Oct 18, 2026, branch `data/geo`)

The data is split by the first character of the lower-case name, because a single file would be 6.0 MB raw / 896 KB gzip, over the ~400 KB limit. Built by `tools/build_geo_index.py` from the points files and zcta_xref.
Format: each file calls `__geoIndex([[name,type,stateAbbr,key],...])`, sorted by lower-case name.
